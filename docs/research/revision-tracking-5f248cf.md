# Does the engine notice what a revision changed?

Measured 2026-09-08 on prompt `5f248cf`. Two drafts of one essay — the same student's first version and the revision that followed it — three reads each, all six on `gemini-3.6-flash`.

The question is not how many cards come back. It is whether the findings move in the direction the writing moved. A tool whose job is to answer "is this better than what I had" is worth little if its output is the same either way.

## The two drafts

The revision closed the essay's largest gap: the first version ends the systematic-review scene on "I didn't bring up the pattern I'd noticed" and never says why, and the revision explains it — "some part of me had already decided it wasn't my place". It paid for that by compressing the front half. The opening lost its two-step ("I assumed I was doing it wrong. It took longer to accept the rule was false"), the laboratory lost "I told my supervisor, took the correction", the pine-green flask went, and a new declarative paragraph arrived: "death is absolute, so the only choice was spending the time before it on something I'd be proud of".

## What the reads did

| Passage | First version | Revision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The silence about the finding | **3 of 3 runs**, twice as `structural/high` | 1 of 3 runs |
| The laboratory / ruined sample | 2 of 3 runs | **3 of 3 runs** |
| "death is absolute…" | not in this draft | 2 of 3 runs |
| The closing claim | 3 of 3, `substantive/high` | weaker — `polish/medium` in one run |

Four passages, four movements, each in the direction the draft actually moved. The repaired gap stops being reported. The passage that lost its agency starts being reported in every run. The paragraph that did not exist before appears. The ending, improved but not fixed, drops in weight rather than disappearing.

## What this does not show

Six reads of one essay by one student. It shows the findings track this revision; it does not establish that they track revisions in general, and a second essay would be the obvious next check.

Nothing here says the cards are *right* — only that they respond to change. Whether "Procedural narration" is the correct name for what the compressed laboratory scene lost is a separate judgement, made by a person.

Card counts were 3, 4, 4 for the first version and 3, 3, 3 for the revision, so this is not visible in the numbers at all. Only the anchors show it, which is an argument for reading the cards rather than counting them.

## Incidental

The recovery pass fired in all three reads of the first version and restored one, two and one lost candidate. Without it those reads would have shown 2, 2 and 3 cards instead of 3, 4 and 4 — and in run 2 the finding on the ending, present in every other read of that draft, would have been among the missing.
