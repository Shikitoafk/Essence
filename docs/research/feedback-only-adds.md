# The engine can only ask for more

Noticed 2026-09-10 by the student using it, while taking one essay from 781
words to 650. Every cut in that revision was made by hand. Across roughly
thirty reads of six drafts, no card ever proposed removing anything.

## What the contract makes possible

Four of the named patterns describe a passage that should shrink or go:

- A motif that repeats without adding
- Detail that decorates instead of working
- Episodes that repeat one facet
- An ending that claims more than it earned

But every card must carry a question, and a question is defined as asking for
"one discoverable unit of lived material" — material the draft does not have.
So a finding whose remedy is deletion still leaves through a door marked
*add*. Measured, on the closing line of the same draft:

> **Card:** Generic closing claim — "I never learned to summon fire."
> **Question:** How did asking the question underneath your data give you a
> sense of agency that "summoning fire" never did?

The diagnosis is that the ending decorates. The instruction is to explain it
at greater length.

## Why it does not converge

`Underdeveloped change` appeared in fourteen of fifteen reads across six
drafts, and moved each time. Asked what the writer said when he finally spoke
up, the draft was given the sentence; the next read asked what happened after
he said it; the read after that asked what he did with it once the room moved
on. Each answer is new text, and new text is a new place for something to be
missing. At 650 of 650 words there is nothing left to spend, so the last of
those questions could not be answered at all.

## The blind spot behind it

The engine treats a draft as extensible. A personal statement is a fixed
container: every finding is a proposal to spend words the student does not
have, and none of them say what to spend.

Related and already recorded: a read of an 781-word draft against a 650-word
limit raised no card about the overrun, though the limit is in the request.
One read has since been seen reasoning about the budget — it dropped a
finding because "the 650-word budget" excused the passage — which is the
behaviour wanted, arrived at once, by accident.

## What to try

1. **A question shaped like a cut.** For the four patterns above: not "tell me
   more", but "what does this paragraph add that the ones around it do not?"
   — with "nothing" a legal answer that leads to deleting it.
2. **A finding that names its price.** On a draft at or over the limit, a card
   asking for new material says what it would displace, or ranks below cards
   that cost nothing.
3. **The budget orders the queue.** Under the limit, ask for material. At or
   over it, what can go comes first.

## What is missing before any of this can be measured

All twelve evaluation cases are about something absent from a draft. None is
about something that should leave one, and none is over its word limit. A
prompt change aimed at this cannot be validated against them.

The same gap appeared one level down on the same day: the question-shape fix
measured well on the cases (median 18 → 14 words, menus 12% → 0%) and barely
moved the real essay (median 20, menus 18%), because the cases are about
activities and the essay is about an interior state. The cases do not contain
the writing that breaks the engine.
